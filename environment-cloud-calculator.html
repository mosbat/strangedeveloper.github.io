Data centers and your company’s carbon impact

Peer‑reviewed and industry research shows that rapidly growing data centers and cloud services are a significant contributor to global electricity use and related CO₂ emissions, especially as AI and cloud adoption accelerate (International Energy Agency, 2024; de Vries‑Gao, 2025; US data‑centre environmental burden studies, 2024).

Why this matters

Studies highlight that data centers now account for a notable share of global energy demand and emissions, with growth driven by cloud computing and AI workloads. This means your company’s cloud decisions—regions, architectures, and utilization—have real environmental consequences.

Sources: International Energy Agency (2024, Energy and AI – Analysis); Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (2024, US Data Center Energy Usage Report); recent peer‑reviewed data‑centre footprint studies.

Lower emissions, stronger financial performance

Empirical evidence shows that companies which actively reduce their carbon emissions tend to report better financial outcomes—specifically higher ROA and ROE—especially when they start from carbon‑intensive operations (Ibishova, Misund & Tveterås, 2024).
